Soundtrail saves songs as private, location-based pins on an in-app map, and it turns how you feel into a mix from your own library.

Music comes from two places, both of them yours: your own Jellyfin server, and audio files you attach yourself. Everything plays inside Soundtrail — nothing is handed to another app.

Features:
* Pins: save a song to a place and see it on the map. Map tiles load only for the area on screen.
* Playlists and automations: a playlist can start near a saved spot, while you walk, run or drive, or within a time window. The opt-in watch shows a notification, and nothing plays without a tap.
* Mood mixes on your device: describe how you feel, or tap a quick pick. A small, explainable engine scores the songs in your own pins and playlists. Optional model providers are off by default, and you approve each destination before any request is sent.
* Jellyfin: sign in to your own server to search and play its music, and download tracks your account is allowed to get. Playback uses your server's authenticated stream, with direct play first and a transcode fallback.
* Your own audio: attach audio files you own or are allowed to use. They are copied into private app storage and play offline, with no server and no account.
* Song lookup: TheAudioDB finds exact artist, album and genre details with no account. Metadata only — it never provides audio.
* Backups: automatic on-device snapshots (the newest 10 are kept), plus JSON export and import.
* Optional end-to-end encrypted cloud sync, using the public server or your own self-hosted one. See and sign out your other devices, change your password, and let the session renew itself before it expires.
* Android Auto: browse your pins and playlists and play them in the car.

Privacy: pins, coordinates, playlists and automations stay on the device unless you turn on cloud sync. With sync on, they are encrypted before upload with a 256-bit recovery key only you hold. The app has no ads, analytics or tracking libraries, and it contains no Google Play services code.

Network: HTTPS is required for every endpoint except a Jellyfin server on your own machine or network, which is enforced in the code and not only in the manifest. Certificates are validated against the system trust store; nothing is pinned, so a self-hosted server can rotate its certificate without breaking the app. No request is ever sent to a server the app did not choose — redirects are not followed.

Requires Android 8.0 or newer.
